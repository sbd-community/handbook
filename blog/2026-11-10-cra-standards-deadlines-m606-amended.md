On 10 November 2026, the European Commission amended the Cyber-Resilience
Act (CRA) standardisation request M/606 with Implementing Decision
C(2026)7861
([decision](https://ec.europa.eu/transparency/documents-register/api/files/C%282026%297861_0/de00000001090398),
[annex](https://ec.europa.eu/transparency/documents-register/api/files/C%282026%297861_1/de00000001090401)).
It gives CEN, CENELEC and ETSI new deadlines: two horizontal standards by
30 November 2026 and all product-category standards by 31 January 2027.
For manufacturers, these dates show when the reference methods for CRA
conformity will arrive.

<!-- truncate -->

The amendment changes when standards are due, not what they must contain.
It does not cite any standard in the Official Journal, and it does not change
CRA application dates. Main CRA obligations still apply from
11 December 2027.

## What changed

Article 1 of the decision replaces Annex I of the original request,
Implementing Decision C(2025)618 of 3 February 2025. The recitals say CEN,
CENELEC and ETSI reported developments affecting timing in their joint
report of 3 June 2026, and that the Commission considered it appropriate to
"slightly adjust" certain 2026 deadlines. The Commission's
[CRA standardisation page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-standardisation)
says the deadline for all vertical standards and some horizontal standards
moves by three months.

The new Annex I sets these adoption deadlines for the European standards
organisations:

| Deliverables | Subject | Deadline |
|--------------|---------|----------|
| 1 | Horizontal: designing, developing and producing products with an appropriate level of cybersecurity based on the risks | 30 November 2026 |
| 15 | Horizontal: vulnerability handling | 30 November 2026 |
| 16–41 | Vertical: product-category standards for important and critical products | 31 January 2027 |
| 2–14 | Horizontal: security properties, such as secure by default, security updates, data minimisation and attack surface | 30 October 2027 |

The vertical list covers the product categories in the CRA's Annexes III and
IV. It runs from identity management systems, browsers, password managers,
operating systems, routers and boot managers (item 23) to microcontrollers,
smart-home products, connected toys, wearables, firewalls, smartcards and
secure elements.

## The acceptance condition

Article 2 adds a condition. If CEN, CENELEC or ETSI indicate that they do not
accept the amendments within a month of receiving the decision, it ceases to
apply, and the request continues in its version of 3 February 2025. So the
new dates hold only if all three organisations accept them.

## Why this matters for connected-product teams

The dates tell you when to expect the reference methods your conformity
assessment will lean on.

- **Vulnerability handling (item 15).** The standard for the CRA's ongoing
  obligations is due by 30 November 2026.
- **Vertical standards (items 16–41).** If your product is an important or
  critical product, its category standard is now due by 31 January 2027.
  That includes the 17 ETSI drafts that went to public enquiry in August,
  which we covered in
  [ETSI Opens Enquiry on 17 CRA Product Standards](/blog/2026/08/13/etsi-cra-vertical-standards-enquiry),
  among them EN 304 623 for boot managers.
- **Property standards (items 2–14).** These are due by 30 October 2027,
  only weeks before the CRA's main obligations apply on 11 December 2027.

Adoption by the standards organisations is not the end of the road.
A standard gives a presumption of conformity only once it is cited in the
Official Journal. With some deadlines this close to December 2027, plan on
meeting the essential requirements directly where a standard is late.

## What to do next

1. Identify which Annex I deliverables apply to your products, especially
   whether a vertical standard covers your product's core function.
2. Put 30 November 2026 and 31 January 2027 in your compliance plan as
   checkpoints for vulnerability handling and product-category standards.
3. Keep working from the current drafts, and record which version you
   assessed against.
4. For the property standards due in October 2027, plan to show conformity
   with the essential requirements directly, using your risk assessment.

## The practical takeaway

The schedule has slipped by three months for the most product-specific
standards, and the property standards land just before the CRA applies.
Don't wait for the final texts. Use the drafts now, and keep your own
evidence that you meet the essential requirements.
