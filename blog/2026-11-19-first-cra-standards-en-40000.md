On 18 November 2026, the first European standards supporting the
**Cyber Resilience Act (CRA)** were published: **EN 40000-1-1** (vocabulary)
and **EN 40000-1-2** (principles, product risk management and lifecycle
activities)
([CEN-CENELEC](https://www.cencenelec.eu/news-events/news/2026/en-in-the-spotlight/2026-11-19-cra-standards-en-40000-1-1-and-en-40000-1-2/),
[European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/first-european-standard-published-support-cyber-resilience-act)).
They give every manufacturer a common starting point, whatever the product.

<!-- truncate -->

Both standards were developed by CEN-CENELEC JTC 13 under the Commission's
standardisation request M/606. CEN and CENELEC announced them on
19 November. They are available through national standards bodies, subject
to each country's publication schedule.

## What was published

**EN 40000-1-1: Vocabulary.** Shared terms for the rest of the EN 40000
series. This sounds minor, but it matters. Many CRA arguments come down to
what "product", "component", "vulnerability" or "support period" means in a
given context.

**EN 40000-1-2: Principles, product risk management and lifecycle
activities.** The horizontal process standard. It covers cybersecurity risk
management and lifecycle activities from design through to decommissioning.
The Commission notes that its scope runs from routers and microcontrollers to
operating systems, browsers, password managers and cloud-based data
processing.

Neither announcement says whether these two standards will be cited in the
Official Journal. Under CRA Article 27, only cited harmonised standards give a
presumption of conformity. Until then, treat them as the agreed reference
method, not a compliance shortcut.

## What comes next

CEN and CENELEC say more is coming soon:

- **FprEN 40000-1-3** on vulnerability handling,
- further horizontal standards on generic security requirements, and
- the first product-category (vertical) standards, alongside ETSI's 17 drafts
  that went to public enquiry in August.

The Commission says more than 30 CRA standards are in preparation.

## Why this matters for connected-product teams

The CRA's essential requirements depend on a cybersecurity risk assessment.
EN 40000-1-2 is the first published, consensus description of what that
risk-management and lifecycle process should look like. It gives engineering
teams a way to structure their process, and gives assessors a reference to
check it against.

It also gives teams a common vocabulary to use in technical files, supplier
contracts and internal policies. That will save arguments later.

## What to do next

1. Buy EN 40000-1-2 from your national standards body and compare it with
   your current threat modeling and risk assessment process.
2. Map your lifecycle artifacts, such as threat models, design reviews,
   release gates and end-of-life plans, to its activities.
3. Adopt EN 40000-1-1 terms in your technical documentation templates.
4. Watch for FprEN 40000-1-3. Vulnerability handling is where the CRA's
   ongoing obligations sit.

## The bigger picture

The CRA standards program has moved from drafts to published text. The
process standards come first and the product-specific ones follow. That is
the right order for teams too: settle your risk and lifecycle process now,
then tune it to your product category as the vertical standards arrive.
