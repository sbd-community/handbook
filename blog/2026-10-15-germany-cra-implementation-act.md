On 15 October 2026, the German Bundestag passed the act implementing the
**Cyber Resilience Act (CRA)** in Germany
([Bundestag](https://www.bundestag.de/dokumente/textarchiv/2026/kw42-de-cyberresilienz-1224508)).
It makes the Federal Office for Information Security (BSI) the central
authority for CRA market surveillance. For manufacturers selling into
Germany, it shows who will check their CRA compliance, and when.

<!-- truncate -->

The CRA is a regulation, so its obligations apply directly in every Member
State. National acts decide the enforcement machinery: which authority
inspects products, who notifies conformity assessment bodies, and how fines
are imposed. Germany is one of the largest markets for connected products, so
its choices matter well beyond German manufacturers.

## What the act does

The Bundestag adopted the bill in the version amended by its Committee on
Internal Affairs (Drucksache 21/8622). According to the Bundestag, it allows
the BSI to act as:

- the national **market surveillance authority** for the CRA, and
- the **notifying authority** for CRA conformity assessment bodies.

The act also contains rules on fine proceedings. Law firm Hogan Lovells
reports that it adds a new part to the BSI Act covering products with digital
elements. It also gives the BSI a CSIRT role and a support function for SMEs
and open-source stewards, and sets fine levels that mirror the CRA's own
maximums
([Hogan Lovells](https://hlc.com/en/publications/eu-cyber-resilience-act-germany-adopts-cra-implementation-act-as-national-enforcement-regimes)).

The committee's last-minute changes concerned when individual provisions take
effect. Secondary reporting says the authority and reporting provisions apply
from the day after promulgation, with the rest following on 11 December 2027,
when the CRA applies in full. The bill now goes to the Bundesrat.

## Why this matters for connected-product teams

A single, central authority is good news for consistency. The government
considered spreading CRA market surveillance across Germany's existing
sector regulators and rejected it, citing the BSI's cybersecurity expertise.
So most connected products will deal with one regulator with deep technical
expertise. The BSI co-authored this year's international
[SBOM minimum elements](/blog/2026/07/29/cisa-2026-sbom-minimum-elements),
so expect technical questions rather than paperwork checks.

The notifying-authority role matters too. Products in the CRA's higher-risk
classes need third-party assessment, and notified bodies cannot start work
until national authorities designate them.

## What to do next

1. If Germany is a significant market, add the BSI to your regulatory
   contacts and watch for its CRA guidance.
2. If your main EU establishment is in Germany, check how CRA reports are
   routed to the German CSIRT through ENISA's Single Reporting Platform.
3. If your products need third-party assessment, track which German bodies
   apply for notification and plan capacity early.
4. Keep your technical file ready to send on request. Market surveillance
   starts with documents.

## The bigger picture

Member States must have CRA authorities and notified bodies ready well before
December 2027. Germany is one of the first large markets to settle its
approach. Expect other national acts to follow, and differences in how they
enforce to show up in the first market surveillance campaigns.

:::note[Later update]
On 20 November 2026, the Bundesrat decided at its 1070th plenary session not to call the mediation committee on the act (Drucksache 621/26), so it has completed its passage through parliament.
